CueComm - install

1. Drag CueComm into the Applications folder.
2. Open it from Applications.

If macOS says CueComm "cannot be opened" or "cannot be verified":
the app is not notarized by Apple, so it needs to be allowed once.

  Option A:  Right-click (or Control-click) CueComm in Applications, choose Open,
             then click Open in the dialog.

  Option B:  Open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click
             "Open Anyway" next to CueComm.

  Option C:  In Terminal, run:
             xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/CueComm.app

On first launch macOS asks for permission to use the Microphone, Speech Recognition
and the Local Network. Allow all three: they are needed for transcription and
for sharing sessions between Macs.

Macs on different networks or subnets (including over the internet):
Bonjour only works inside one subnet. On the Mac that hosts the session, open Session
and copy the address under "Others can join this session at". On the other Mac, open
Session, paste it under "Join by IP address" and press Join. That Mac joins the
session straight away, you do not need to type the session name. Over the internet,
forward TCP port 47800 on the router of the Mac being called, or put both Macs on a
VPN such as Tailscale and use its address. Connections are not encrypted.

Soundcards and Dante Virtual Soundcard:
Choose the soundcard by name in each channel's settings instead of "System default": the
default can switch to a one-input microphone and the channel would then lose its input.
The channel's settings show the peak level of the selected input in dBFS. "Even out the
level" and "Ignore noise between speech" prepare the audio for the recogniser; use the gain
slider on a very quiet send. If the channel's status line reports dropouts, raise the
latency in the Dante Virtual Soundcard window.

Requires macOS 14 or newer (macOS 15 or newer for translation).
