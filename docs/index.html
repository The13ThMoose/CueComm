ChannelsQ14 · 0:31

Every channel is a colour. Every word has a time.

Give every source its own channel: monitors, FOH, the show caller, a stage position. CueComm turns what is said on each one into a chat-style transcript: who said what, and when, in the channel’s own colour.

Words you care about are marked in yellow. If one arrives while you are looking at another channel, that channel’s badge turns yellow with a flag, so a request is never missed.

A channel for every source
Name it, colour it, pin it, group it. On a Mac each channel listens to its own input on your audio interface, with a live level meter to confirm you picked the right one. On Android a device speaks into one channel at a time.
Keywords
Mark words per channel. Matches are highlighted in the transcript and flagged on the channel.
Translation
Turn translation on per channel and the translation sits under the original. Apple’s Translation does it on a Mac with macOS 15 or later; on-device models do it on Android.
Type back
Type a reply into any channel, and everyone sharing it sees it.
Export and share a setup
Export a transcript as text or CSV. Save your channels, groups and cues as a setup file; the Mac and Android apps read the same file.
CuesQ29 · 1:07

Say the word. OSC goes out, every screen flashes.

A cue word is a word with two jobs. When it is heard, CueComm sends an OSC message to a lighting desk or show-control system, and flashes the screen. Add words like GO and STANDBY, and set the OSC address and the colour of the flash.

In a session the flash goes to every Mac and Android device, so the director sees the cue even when the word was said on another device. OSC goes out from the device that heard the word, so your desk gets one GO, not one for every device.

OSC out
Send OSC over UDP to any host and port, with the address and arguments you choose. The text, the channel and the keyword can be part of the message.
OSC in
Listen for incoming OSC and let it trigger the same actions. On a Mac, MIDI in and out work too.
Automations
Triggers: a word heard, any message, an incoming OSC message (and MIDI on a Mac). Actions: send OSC (and MIDI on a Mac), flash, select a channel, scroll, clear, mark as read, change the text size.
Flash every device
On by default for a flash; switch it off per cue. Each device can also choose to ignore flashes from the others.
SessionsQ50 · 1:58

One session. Macs and phones, on any network.

Host a session on any Mac or Android device. Devices on the same network find it by themselves: tap Join. Everyone sees the same channels and transcript, except channels you mark private, and a device that joins late catches up with the recent transcript.

A device on another network or subnet joins by IP address: type the address of any device in the session under Join by IP address and you are in that session at once. No session name to type, no waiting. If the link drops, it reconnects by itself.

Same network
Devices find each other automatically.
Another network
Type the host’s address and port, for example 192.168.1.20:47800. The default port is 47800.
Over the internet
Forward TCP port 47800 on the host’s router, or put the devices on a VPN such as Tailscale. Connections are not encrypted, so use a VPN on networks you do not trust.
Mac and Android together
One session can mix both. Each device connects straight to every other one: there is no server, no account and no relay.

    CueComm asks for the microphone and speech recognition, and for Local Network access to find other devices. The rest of the app works without them.

Prefer Terminal? Remove the quarantine flag instead:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/CueComm.app

Why the warning?

Notarisation needs a paid Apple developer account, which this beta does not have yet. To check that the file is the one published here, compare its SHA-256 with the line on the left:

QuestionsQ87 · 3:26

Questions

Is it free?

Yes, while it is in beta. What happens after the beta will be announced here.

Which devices does it run on?

Macs with macOS 14 or later, Intel or Apple Silicon, and Android phones and tablets with Android 8.0 or later. There is no iPhone or iPad version yet. This is a beta: so far it has been tested on an Apple Silicon Mac and in an Android 15 emulator, and real phones and Intel Macs are still to be tried.

Do the devices have to be on the same network?

No. On one network they find each other. On different networks, one device types the other’s IP address and joins its session. Over the internet that needs a forwarded port or a VPN.

Is the connection encrypted?

No. Sessions use plain TCP, with no server in between. On a network you do not trust, put the devices on a VPN such as Tailscale.

Which audio interface can I use?

On a Mac, any CoreAudio interface: each channel listens to its own input. On Android, a phone listens through one microphone at a time, so it speaks into one channel; a USB or Bluetooth microphone that Android uses as its input works as usual.

Which lighting or show-control systems does it work with?

Any system that accepts OSC over UDP. You choose the host, port, address and arguments for each cue.

Does anything leave my device?

There is no CueComm server and no account. Transcripts and cues go only to the other devices in your session. On a Mac, speech recognition is set to run on the device wherever the language supports it; you can change that per channel. On Android, recognition prefers the offline language pack; if it is not installed, the phone falls back to Google’s online recogniser, which sends audio to Google. Translation language packs download once and then run on the device.

Which languages are supported?

Speech: the languages your Mac’s or phone’s own speech recogniser supports; you pick one per channel. Translation: the languages Apple’s Translation (Mac) and the on-device translator (Android) offer. The apps themselves are in English; this page comes in English and Norwegian.

Is the source code available?

Yes, on GitHub. No open-source licence has been chosen yet, so by default you may read it but not reuse it.

Something is wrong. Where do I report it?

Open an issue on GitHub. Say which macOS or Android version you use and what you did.
